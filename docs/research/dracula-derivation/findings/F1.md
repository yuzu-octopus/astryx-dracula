# F1 — Official Dracula spec + dracula-ui: derived vs pinned

## Angle

Whether the OFFICIAL Dracula specification and the dracula/dracula-ui repos prescribe colours to be
DERIVED (tints/shades/washes/opacity ramps/on-fill rules) or PINNED (exact hex literals). Sub-questions:
(a) tint/shade/wash derivation, (b) opacity/alpha conventions for status surfaces, (c) on-fill foreground
choice, (d) sequential/diverging chart ramps, (e) contrast requirements for derived colours.

### C9 — dracula-ui is the one repo with a FULL algorithmic derivation engine, and it is the LEAST spec-compliant.

`dracula-ui` is **not** Less/Sass. It is plain `.css` with a `postcss-each` codegen pass. There is **no `fade(`, no `mix(`, no `lighten(`, no `darken(`, no `color-mix`, no `adjust-hue`, no `saturate(` anywhere in the repo** (`grep -rnE "fade\(|fadein|@?mix\(|lighten\(|darken\(|color-mix|saturate\(|adjust-hue|opacity-mix" --include=*.{css,less,scss,ts,tsx,js,json}` → exit 1, no matches).

`src/styles/colors.css:1-77` — the derivation system, in full:

```css

14:   --whiteLight: hsla(60, 30%, 96%, 5%);
```

The rules, stated exactly:
- **Accent base** = `hsl(H, 100%, 75%)`
- **`-Secondary`** = same H/S at **L 90%** (a +15pt lift)
- **`-Light`** = base at **5% alpha** (the "wash")
- **`-transparent`** = base at **10% alpha**
- **`-{10..90}0`** = base at **N% alpha**, 9-step ladder via `postcss-each`
- **Neutral ramp** = `hsl(230, 15%, L)` at L = 15/30/45/70/75, plus 5% wash; `230°, 15%` is the background hue/sat
- **`--white`** = `hsl(60, 30%, 96%)` — i.e. the Foreground's own HSL

Codegen: `tsdx.config.js:11-25` registers `require('postcss-each')` and `postcss-prefixer({prefix:'drac-'})`.

- Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dracula/dracula-ui/main/tsdx.config.js
- Confidence: **primary**
- Meaning: this is a **complete, systematic, lightness-ramp + alpha-ladder derivation engine** — structurally very close to a HSL lightness ramp. It is also the *only* community Dracula UI that does this. It is sunset (see C13).

## Conflicting evidence

**X1 — "Pin everything" vs "derive from tokens".** Resolved by role, not by contradiction:
- The **palette** is pinned, twice, normatively (`spec.mdx:367`, `:391`).
- The **reference implementation** derives 131 washes from those pinned tokens via `!alpha` (`classic.yml`).
- These are not in conflict: the spec pins the *base tokens*; the reference implementation derives *surfaces* from them. **Pin the base, derive the surfaces** is the reading the primary sources jointly support. Confidence: high.

**X2 — dui's derived palette vs the spec palette.** Unresolvable as "which is right" — they are different palettes. Reconciled on the criterion the spec itself sets: dui's accents are not in the spec's Color Palette (`spec.mdx:14-48`), so dui is non-compliant. dui is also sunset (C13). **The spec wins.** Confidence: high.

**X3 — Two "Alucard" palettes (C19).** Reconciled on date + authority: spec Alucard launched July 2025 per the official 2026 roadmap post; `spec.mdx` is declared "the authoritative reference for Dracula Theme implementations" (closing line). dracula-css's inverted-Classic light mode is a non-compliant convenience layer. **Spec wins.** Confidence: high.

**X4 — dracula-css's `brightness(1.1)` hover vs the 4.5:1 requirement.** The filter is not contrast-tested (the a11y test has no hover case and no colour-vs-colour case). `brightness(1.1)` on `#50FA7B` moves it toward white, which *reduces* its ratio against `#F8F8F2` text — but the button text colour is not `#F8F8F2` on a green fill anyway (see C17). **Flagged as untested, not as a violation.** [inference]

## A2 — Group classification under "pin the base, derive surfaces by alpha off a named token"

**Group A — `--color-background-<hue>` 1A + `--color-border-<hue>` 4D** (`tokens.css:218-237`, 20
tokens / 10 hues) → **KEEP-PIN. Already exactly right.** These ARE alpha-off-a-named-token. `1A` =
10.20% → official 10%; `4D` = 30.20% → official 30%. Mechanism correct, values on-ladder, 9 of 10
bases are genuine spec tokens.
- **Defect:** `--color-background-gray` / `--color-border-gray` = `#9AA1BC1A` / `#9AA1BC4D`. Base
  `#9AA1BC` is NOT a spec token — it is the invented Comment lift our own comment at `tokens.css:7-8`
  admits to. Correct mechanism, **invented base** → rule-D human flag. The spec-anchored alternative
  is Comment `#6272A4`, but that is the exact colour `tokens.css:7` says fails the 4.5:1 body-text
  floor, so it is not a drop-in.

**Group B — `--color-data-*` HSL lightness ramps**, 45 tokens (`tokens.css:269-313`), L = 28/44/60/74/88
→ **CONTESTED SET, but the mechanism is STRUCTURALLY REQUIRED, not a doctrine violation.**
Tested directly. Compositing `a*H + (1-a)*S` over a dark surface always lands DARKER than the pure
hue `H`, so alpha can never exceed the hue's own lightness:

| ramp hue | base | base L | alpha-reachable steps | unreachable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| purple | `#BD93F9` | 78% | 4/5 | L88 |
| cyan | `#8BE9FD` | 77% | 4/5 | L88 |
| yellow | `#F1FA8C` | 76% | 4/5 | L88 |
| green | `#50FA7B` | 65% | 3/5 | L74, L88 |
| blue | `#6272A4` | 51% | 2/5 | L60, L74, L88 |
| gray | `#8C939B` | 58% | 2/5 | L60, L74, L88 |

A 5-step magnitude ramp needs lightness in BOTH directions; alpha over a dark ground only supplies
one. **The ramps do not break the doctrine — the doctrine does not cover ramps.** Present to the
human as "needs its own rule", not as "violates the alpha rule". Per C8 there is also no official
precedent for ANY sequential/diverging ramp. Separately, `blue` (S27%) and `gray` (S7%) are poor ramp
bases and will look muddy at 5 steps — a quality question separate from the mechanism.

**Group C — AA text lifts** `#9AA1BC` / `#B0B3C4` / `#8288A6` (`tokens.css:196-202`) → **KEEP-PIN on
mechanism, CONTESTED on provenance.** Alpha would be actively wrong here: these are text colours
required to clear 4.5:1, and compositing toward a dark surface REDUCES contrast, so alpha would break
the exact requirement they exist to satisfy. But all are hand-cut hexes with no spec anchor — the
spec offers only Comment `#6272A4` in that band. Same rule-D flag as Group A's gray.

### A5.1 — Overlay hover: the official convention and ours both lose, for opposite reasons

`#F8F8F2` text contrast, delta in contrast points (negative = worse for text):

| surface | base | official `!alpha[*Foreground,10]` (lighten) | ours `#0000001F` hover (darken 12.16%) | ours `#00000033` pressed (darken 20%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| body #282A36 | 13.36:1 | 9.87:1 (**−3.48**) | 14.31:1 (+0.95) | 14.91:1 (+1.56) |
| surface #343746 | 11.06:1 | 8.21:1 (**−2.85**) | 12.23:1 (+1.17) | 13.01:1 (+1.95) |
| popover #424450 | 9.06:1 | 6.84:1 (**−2.22**) | 10.32:1 (+1.26) | 11.20:1 (+2.14) |
| muted #44475A | 8.59:1 | 6.52:1 (**−2.06**) | 9.85:1 (+1.26) | 10.74:1 (+2.16) |

The official lighten costs 2.06–3.48 points on every tier. Our darken GAINS only ~1 point while
moving body `#282A36` to `#23252F` — too small to read as feedback. Neither background channel works.

**The transferable caveat (A5.4) is the important part of this.**

### A5.2 — The ring channel: ceiling analysis kills Comment as a non-text affordance

`--shadow-inset-hover` (`tokens.css:159`) is `inset 0 0 0 2px #6272A430` = Comment `#6272A4` at
18.82%. A wash moves a surface only a fraction of the way toward the ring colour, so the **ceiling**
is the pure-colour ratio and no alpha can exceed it. WCAG 1.4.11 non-text contrast needs 3:1.

| ring colour | ceiling vs #343746 | alpha needed for 3:1 | popover | muted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Comment `#6272A4` | **2.51:1** | **NEVER — unreachable at any alpha** | 2.05:1 NEVER | 1.94:1 NEVER |
| Functional Purple `#815CD6` | **2.49:1** | **NEVER** | 2.04:1 NEVER | 1.93:1 NEVER |
| Purple `#BD93F9` | 4.89:1 | 65.7% (`0xA7`) | 4.00:1 @76.4% | 3.79:1 @79.9% |
| Cyan `#8BE9FD` | 8.52:1 | 44.7% (`0x72`) | 6.98:1 @50.0% | 6.61:1 @51.7% |
| AnsiBright `#A4FFFF` | 10.29:1 | 39.8% (`0x65`) | 8.43:1 @44.0% | 7.99:1 @45.4% |

**This is stronger than "fails at every alpha in the family".** Comment cannot reach 3:1 on the
surface tier at ANY alpha including 100%. So the alpha is not the defect and the family cannot be
rescued by repinning the value — the **colour** is wrong. Both dead entries are the
Comment/desaturated-blue family, which is the same colour already demoted out of body text for
failing 4.5:1.

Honest limit: a 65.7% Purple ring is a heavy outline, probably too loud for most surfaces. There may
be a good reason to ship a subtle ring that technically fails 1.4.11. That is a design judgement, not
a measurement, and it is **not mine to make**. Nobody may describe `--shadow-inset-hover` as meeting
1.4.11 in any doc or comment.

### A5.3 — Heat-scale families: measured on both axes that matter

Ramp steps L = 28/44/60/74/88. Two axes: adjacent-step separation (is the ramp readable?) and
darkest-step-vs-body (can "low" be seen at all?).

| family | steps | min adjacent sep | darkest vs body #282A36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| shamrock (spec Green `#50FA7B`) | `#048B26 #06DA3C #39F969 #7EFB9E #C4FDD2` | 1.08:1 | **3.20:1 PASSES** |
| teal `#8BE9FD` (as shipped) | `#02748C #04B7DD #36D9FC #7DE6FD #C3F4FE` | 1.17:1 | 2.64:1 fails |
| teal `#A4FFFF` (post-repin) | `#00778F #00BBE0 #33DDFF #7AE9FF #C2F5FF` | 1.15:1 | 2.74:1 fails |
| purple `#BD93F9` | `#3C0887 #5E0CD5 #893EF4 #B281F8 #DCC5FC` | **1.58:1 best** | **1.07:1 worst** |

1. **Shamrock is the only non-forbidden family whose darkest step clears 3:1 against the body.** A
   heat cell reading "low" on a teal ramp sits ~2.7:1 from the surface behind it — the same class of
   failure as the inset ring, on the data axis instead of the interaction axis.
2. **The purple sequential ramp is confirmed SANCTIONED and DOCUMENTED**, verified in the repo:
   `references/visual.md:11` states in one sentence that `CHART_HUES` is "purple-free by
   construction — purple means tappable, never data" AND that "Sequential ramps cover 9 families
   (purple, pink, red, orange, yellow, teal, blue, shamrock, gray)"; `SKILL.md:184-186` names purple
   first. So the question is **module rule vs token rule**, not "is purple banned". The six purple
   data tokens ship (`--color-data-categorical-purple` + `--color-data-purple-5..1`); only
   `shared/chart-hues.ts:2-3` keeps purple out of charts. The human decides whether to keep or
   revoke the documented distinction.
3. **The purple numbers are arithmetical, not aesthetic.** Purple has the BEST adjacent separation
   and the WORST darkest-step separation, because purple is the one family whose lightest step is
   its own base hue — walking the reserved-for-interaction accent down 5 steps necessarily produces
   a 1.07:1 low end. The ban and the maths agree.
4. **The real trade-off is status, not pretty.** Shamrock clears the floor but shamrock IS spec
   Green, already the success hue, so a data ramp in it risks reading as "good" rather than "more".
   Teal is distinguishable from status but its low end is weak. Nothing wins both axes. That is a
   judgement about what the charts are FOR — **the human's call, not mine.**

### A5.4 — The caveat on our own doctrine, which belongs beside it and not buried

`!alpha [*Foreground, N]` is tuned for VS Code chrome, where hover brightens a **chrome item** and
the text IS that item. Our hover is a wash **under text that stays put**. Same syntax, different
geometry — so the official convention does not transfer to our case without inverting.

Therefore: the doctrine is correct for **fills and washes** (what it was derived for; the 10%/30%
wash spine measures 6.58:1 minimum across every hue and surface), and **wrong as a blanket rule for
interaction states**, where geometry decides whether alpha costs text contrast. Recorded because
"derive by alpha" is exactly the kind of rule the next agent will apply past its scope.
