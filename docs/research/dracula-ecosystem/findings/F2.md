that are NOT in our 29-px base list; which of those are spec-sanctioned vs port-invented; how

diverges from the free spec.

### C14 — Where Dracula's own Vim port diverges from the free spec
`dracula/vim` is first-party (`dracula` org) and is the authoritative consumer. Divergences found at HEAD `e7817b4`:

1. **Adds a 5th surface step.** `autoload/dracula.vim:10` `bgdarker ['#191A21', 234]` — the spec has 4 rows; the port promotes `Background Darker` into the working palette and *uses* it (`dracula_base.vim:157` `DraculaBgDarker`, and `:211` `DraculaWinSeparator`/`:210` `DraculaBoundary`/`:223` `DraculaInlayHint` sit on it).
2. **Collapses `bglighter` and `subtle`.** `autoload/dracula.vim:6` `bglighter #424450` and `:14` `subtle #424450` are the same value under two names. So `FoldColumn`, `NonText`, and `CursorLine` (`:239`) all resolve to `#424450`, *not* to `#343746`.
3. **CursorLine = Subtle = `#424450`, not the spec's translucent Current Line.** `dracula_base.vim:239`. The spec (`spec.mdx:52-57`) says use `#6272A4` as a translucent overlay, or `#353747` opaque. The port uses neither.
4. **No contrast claim anywhere.** `doc/dracula.txt` (198 lines) and `README.md` document `g:dracula_bold`, `_italic`, `_underline`, `_undercurl`, `_inverse`, `_colorterm`, `_high_contrast_diff`, `_full_special_attrs_support` — and **never mention a contrast ratio or WCAG**. The spec's 4.5:1 statement (`spec.mdx:255`) has no port-side counterpart.
5. **Inverts the diff model on a flag.** `g:dracula_high_contrast_diff` (`doc/dracula.txt:100-103`) does not raise contrast — it swaps to a *different* palette (Yellow-on-Purple, bgdark-on-Red). It is the only first-party place the palette is offered in two variants.
6. **Terminal colors are opt-in via terminal capability, not via `:terminal`.** `dracula_base.vim:28-30` bails entirely unless `termguicolors` or GUI or 256-colour; `:56-67` sets `g:terminal_color_N` (nvim) and `g:terminal_ansi_colors` (vim). `g:dracula_colorterm` (`:233`) controls whether *background fills* are emitted at all — a first-party acknowledgment that some terminals cannot do opaque backgrounds.

### C15 — Emacs is the only port that documents a derivation from a spec colour, and the derivation it documents is wrong
`dracula/emacs/dracula-theme.el:221-223`:
```
(dark-red   "#880000" "#870000" "red") ; 40% darker official variant
(dark-green "#037a22" "#00af00" "green") ; 40% darker official variant
(dark-blue  "#0189cc" "#0087d7" "brightblue")
```
These are the only hexes anywhere in the ecosystem shipped with a *provenance comment naming the
palette*. The comment says "40% darker **official** variant". I tested it against both plausible
bases — the **syntax** colours the hexes actually match in H/S, and the spec's **Functional**
colours the word "official" would most naturally point at. It matches neither consistently:

| pair | H | S | L | L ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `#FF5555` → `#880000` | 0.0 → 0.0 | 100 → 100 | 66.7 → 26.7 | **0.400** |
| `#50FA7B` → `#037A22` | 135.2 → 135.6 | 94.4 → 95.2 | 64.7 → 24.5 | 0.379 |
| `#8BE9FD` → `#0189CC` | 190.5 → 199.8 | 96.6 → 99.0 | 76.9 → 40.2 | 0.523 |

Against the spec's **Functional** colours (`#DE5735`, `#089108`, `#0081D6` — `spec.mdx:135-138`) the L ratios are 0.495 / 0.817 / 0.958 and the hues drift, i.e. nothing like 0.4. The H/S columns above show which base the hexes *actually* descend from: `#880000` is a pure hue-0 red like spec Red `#FF5555`, not like Functional Red `#DE5735` (H 12.1).
- Measured with `colorsys.rgb_to_hls` on the literal hexes, 2026-09-28.
- **Conclusion: red is exactly HSL-L × 0.400 of spec Red `#FF5555`; green is × 0.379; blue is × 0.523 and drifts 9.3° in hue. The "40% darker official variant" comment is accurate for one of three, approximate for one, and wrong for one.**
- Confidence: **primary** (arithmetic on cited hexes), **primary** (that the comment's claim does not hold for all three)

## Conflicting evidence

**1. What is AnsiBrightGreen?** Four answers across the ecosystem: `#69FF94` (spec + 4 first-party
ports), `#5AF78E` (terminal-app, xresources, and alacritty's *normal* green, not bright), `#00AF00`
(emacs 256-column), `#50FA7B` (iterm has no bright green — Ansi 10 is the normal green).
**Resolution**: the spec value `#69FF94` wins. It is the only one with 5 independent first-party
corroborations and a spec line number (`spec.mdx:79`). The off-spec family splits further:
alacritty takes `#555555` (bright black) and `#BBBBBB` (Ansi 7) from the iTerm lineage but
`#CAA9FA` (bright blue) from the terminal-app/xresources lineage, and its bright *red* is
`#FF5555` — byte-identical to its normal red. Alacritty's file is itself a mix of the two
lineages, so treat the whole off-spec bright family as one artifact with two sub-lineages,
not as consensus.
**2. What is the background?** `#282A36` (spec, and every editor port) vs `#1E1F29` (iterm, and
Terminal.app's `BackgroundColor`, and JetBrains' caret row). **Resolution**: `#282A36` is the spec
value. Measured, not estimated — `grep -rIl -iE '#282a36|282a36' <repo> --exclude-dir=.git | wc -l`
returns ≥1 for **13 of the 15** repos cloned (vim, visual-studio-code, jetbrains, emacs, sublime,
textmate, micro, kakoune, kitty, xresources, alacritty-theme, atom, i3) and **0 for exactly two**:
`dracula/iterm` and `dracula/terminal-app`. Those two are the only ports in the set that lack the
spec background. `#1E1F29` is therefore a two-port lineage, and a *candidate* only in the sense
that two independent files contain it.

**3. What is "non-text"?** Comment `#6272A4` (vim `LineNr`/`SignColumn`) vs Background Light
`#343746` (kakoune `non_text`) vs `!alpha [WHITE, 10]` (vscode `NonText #FFFFFF1A`) vs
`#424450` (vim `NonText`, because vim aliases `subtle` to `bglighter`) vs `#A3A5BA` (JetBrains).
**Resolution**: no reconciliation possible — these are five different jobs collapsed into one name.
`NonText` in vim (`:260`) and `LineNr` in vim (`:258`) are *different colours within one file*, which
is the cleanest evidence that this is not a palette question but a naming question.

**4. How many surface steps?** 1 (alacritty), 2 (iterm), 4 (spec, kakoune, vscode, emacs), 5 (vim,
JetBrains). **Resolution**: the spec defines 4 rows but only 3 *distinct* values, because
"Floating interactive elements" and "Background Light" are both `#343746` (`spec.mdx:113,115`).
Dracula's own Vim port resolves the collision by aliasing `bglighter` = `subtle` = `#424450` and
carrying `#191A21` as a 5th. So the spec's own ambiguity is resolved *in code* by the first-party
port, not in prose.

**5. Is the diff a wash or a colour swap?** VS Code + kakoune: alpha wash, asymmetric (Green 20 /
Red 50). Vim: opaque swap, Red-on-Background-Dark by default, Yellow-on-Purple on a flag. micro:
foreground only. JetBrains: three unrelated hues. **Resolution**: unreconcilable; no spec section
exists (kakoune's own link to `spec.draculatheme.com#sec-Diffs` is a dangling anchor, which is
itself the finding — the port that cites the spec most precisely cites a section that does not
exist).
