# F1 — Dracula PRO: the paid reference ceiling

## Angle

Dracula PRO (commercial) vs the free spec: different palette or same palette better applied; which apps; what is published; what colour practice is documented.

Fetch date for every URL below: **2026-09-28**.

## Claims

### C1 — PRO ships a DIFFERENT palette, not the free palette re-applied
- Official, verbatim, from the PRO page "Geometric Color Palette" card (source of the rendered page text is `dracula/draculatheme.com` `src/components/pro/bento/palette.tsx:1-24`):
  > "Dracula's original colors, created in 2013, were based on personal taste. This new Pro version brings a more refined and *mathematical approach* that normalizes luminosity and saturation."
- Same sentence, independently, in the launch post: "The original colors created in 2013 were based on personal taste. This new Pro version brings a more refined and mathematical approach that **normalizes lightness and saturation**."
- Note the wording differs between the two official sources: the /pro page says **luminosity** and saturation; the 2020 blog says **lightness** and saturation. Same claim, two spellings.
- https://draculatheme.com/pro · https://draculatheme.com/blog/dracula-pro-is-here · repo `dracula/draculatheme.com` (shallow clone, commit on main at 2026-09-28)
- Confidence: **primary** (two independent official artefacts, one being first-party source code).

### C2 — The team's own framing is DERIVATION, not hand-crafting
- The /pro page sells PRO as mathematically derived. The only "hand" language on the page is **Hand-picked Typography**, which is about four monospaced fonts with ligatures, not about colours.
- Any downstream claim that "PRO colours are hand-crafted" inverts the source. What is hand-*picked* in PRO is fonts; what is hand-*crafted* in PRO is application coverage.
- https://draculatheme.com/pro
- Confidence: **primary**.

### C3 — Structural evidence for the geometric claim exists officially, as an image; the VALUES do not
- `public/images/blog/dracula-pro-is-here-a.webp` (in `dracula/draculatheme.com`) is captioned "Color Palette Comparison" and shows two hue wheels, "Dracula" and "Dracula PRO".
- Observed structure: both wheels plot 7 swatches. On the Dracula wheel the 6 small swatches sit at irregular angles and irregular radii from the centre. On the PRO wheel the 6 small swatches sit at visibly regular angular intervals, and the single large primary swatch sits at the bottom of both wheels (≈270°). This is the "mathematical approach" rendered.
- **The pixel values in that image are NOT usable as a colour source.** I converted it and sampled the swatch centres (`ffmpeg` webp→png, Swift `NSBitmapImageRep.colorAt`). Sampled Dracula-wheel red came back `#ED7766` where the true Dracula red is `#FF5555` — the red channel dropped 255→237, so the swatches are composited under a light overlay/streak treatment, not painted flat. Every sample is systematically lightened. I withdrew the samples rather than report contaminated numbers.
- Conclusion: the image is primary evidence for **geometry**, worthless as evidence for **values**.
- Confidence: **primary** for the geometry claim, **methodological** for the withdrawal.

### C4 — PRO states a 4.5:1 contrast floor, against WCAG 2.0 Level AA
- Verbatim, from `src/components/pro/bento/precise-contrast.tsx:1-13` (rendered at https://draculatheme.com/pro):
  > "Battle-tested with WCAG 2.0 Level AA Specifications, with a Contrast Ratio of at least **4.5:1** for standard text, thus providing the *best readability*."
- The page says **WCAG 2.0**, not 2.1 or 2.2. That is a specific version claim and it is old; WCAG 2.2 added 1.4.11 non-text contrast and 1.4.6 AAA headings. PRO's public claim does not cover 2.2.
- Confidence: **primary**.

### C12 — Normalising HSL lightness does NOT normalise contrast — the most transferable finding here
- WCAG relative-luminance contrast of every PRO accent against the PRO background `#22212C`, computed:

| PRO token | Hex | vs `#22212C` |
|---|---|---|
| yellow | `#FFFF80` | 15.03:1 |
| cyan | `#80FFEA` | 13.17:1 |
| green | `#8AFF80` | 12.63:1 |
| red | `#FF9580` | 7.46:1 |
| magenta | `#FF80BF` | 6.88:1 |
| **blue** | `#9580FF` | **5.15:1** |
| brightBlack | `#504C67` | 1.94:1 |
| foreground | `#F8F8F2` | 14.90:1 |

- The spread at *identical* HSL lightness (75.10%) and *identical* saturation (100%) is **5.15:1 to 15.03:1 — a 2.9× range**. Perceptual lightness normalisation delivers nothing to contrast normalisation, because relative luminance is hue-dependent: a blue at 75% HSL lightness is a much darker colour than a yellow at 75% HSL lightness.
- Same computation on the **free** spec, for contrast: Comment `#6272A4` 3.03:1, Red `#FF5555` 4.53:1, Purple `#BD93F9` 5.90:1, Pink `#FF79C6` 5.97:1, Orange `#FFB86C` 8.36:1, Cyan `#8BE9FD` 10.29:1, Green `#50FA7B` 10.38:1, Yellow `#F1FA8C` 12.74:1. Free accent floor 4.53:1; PRO accent floor 5.15:1.
- Direct relevance to the kit under study: the kit's existing 45 HSL ramps already fix lightness per step at constant hue+saturation. C12 is the measured warning that **an HSL ramp is not a contrast ramp** — if a chart ramp must clear a contrast floor at every step, lightness must be solved per-hue against the surface, not shared across families.
- Confidence: **primary arithmetic**, applied to C10's secondary inputs.

### C13 — The surface-tier move is also measurable
- PRO background `#22212C` sits at HSL L=15.10% against the free spec's `#282A36` at L=18.43% — PRO's canvas is roughly 3.3 points darker in HSL, and 0.0161 vs 0.0237 in relative luminance (0.68×).
- The free spec's only surface token, Current Line / Selection `#44475A`, gives **1.56:1** against its own body. PRO's nearest equivalent, brightBlack `#504C67`, gives **1.94:1** against `#22212C` — a measurably stronger, though still very weak, secondary-surface separation.
- So PRO buys its surface separation by pushing the canvas down AND the secondary up, not by adding tiers. No third tier is visible in the public ANSI set.
- Confidence: **derived from secondary data**.

- Product rationale (2020 launch, `draculatheme.com/blog/dracula-pro-is-here`): "It's been almost 7 years since Dracula launched, and we are incredibly excited about this next step. - Business rationale (https://draculatheme.com/pro/journey): "**Focus on premium experience** — The market is saturated with mediocre products. Differentiate yourself by offering superior quality and exceptional support." and "**Consistency beats isolated peaks** — Sales vary daily."

- No primary source found in which the team states "Pro exists because the colours were hand-crafted" or "because of accessibility work". The stated reason is **re-derivation of the palette plus per-app application depth and support**, monetised as a premium tier. The "hand-crafted" framing is third-party, not the team's.
- Confidence: **primary**.

## Conflicting evidence

1. **"normalizes luminosity" vs "normalizes lightness".** The /pro page says luminosity; the 2020 blog says lightness. The measured data (C11) shows HSL lightness is the normalised quantity, so the blog is the accurate one and the page is loose language. Not reconciled by the team either way. Carried forward as: the page's wording is imprecise, the claim holds under the HSL reading.

2. **"20 themes" vs 19 shipped.** See C6. Unresolved.

3. **PRO's public 4.5:1 claim vs PRO's own brightBlack.** The claim is scoped to "standard text" and every PRO accent does clear 4.5:1 (floor 5.15:1, C12). But the surface/selection token brightBlack `#504C67` sits at 1.94:1 against the background — well under the 3:1 WCAG 1.4.11 non-text bar. The claim is not false, it is scoped, and the scope excludes the one token that would fail a non-text check. Recorded, not resolved by the team.

4. **Hand-crafted (community) vs mathematical (official).** Direct contradiction. Official sources win per the primary-source rule; the community framing appears to be an inference from PRO's price. Recorded.

## Gaps

- **No official confirmation of the circulated 16-value ANSI set.** Two independent third-party configs agree exactly, which is strong, but neither traces to the Dracula team. Every PRO hex above stays `unverified — paywalled`. The nearest primary would be `design/PALETTE.md` inside the paid package, or the PRO Figma/Sketch files. Both are behind the Gumroad checkout.
- **No public PRO surface-tier, elevation, hover, disabled, or focus documentation at all.** C16 is a measured absence, not a search failure.
- **No public values for Blade, Buffy, Lincoln, Morbius, Van Helsing.** Not one hex found for any of the five.
- **The PRO Alucard light variant's values are not public**, and per C15 they are deliberately distinct from the open-source Alucard. A sibling worker owns the OSS Alucard palette; nothing here contradicts it, but the PRO light line is a separate, unmeasured thing.
- **The #0E0D11 `#FFCA80` ambiguity is unresolved**: `#FFCA80` sits in a PRO-branded asset but is never named as a palette token. It could be PRO's orange, or it could simply be a yellow chosen for a star. Treating it as a candidate is defensible only as "a verified official-file value with unknown role".
- **The `limeGreen`/`lightCoral` duplicates** in Source A (lines 75-76, repeating `green` and `red`) are Hyper-specific aliases, not palette tokens. No conclusion drawn.
