# Angle 1 — HERO TYPE SCALE SPREAD

## Angle
Marketing-page hero `h1` renders at 24px, 35px, or 42px in the same brand. What is a hero h1 normatively, is our scale a real modular scale, and what is the ONE value the kit should pin?

## Defect (verified in our kit)

Measured census of every `<Heading level={1}>` in `templates/*.tsx` + `shared/*.tsx` + `demo/*.tsx`:

| h1 rendering | px | token | h1 sites |
|---|---|---|---|
| no `type` | 24 | `--text-heading-1-size` → `--font-size-2xl` | **38** |
| `type="display-2"` | 35 | `--text-display-2-size` → `--font-size-4xl` | **12** |
| `type="display-1"` | 42 | `--text-display-1-size` → `--font-size-5xl` | **2** |
| **total** | | | **52** |

Same job, three sizes, one brand. The majority (38/52) is the SMALLEST of the three. `SKILL.md:191` lists `--text-display-1…3` as available tokens but binds no role to any single value, so the kit cannot answer "how big is a hero in this brand".

Adjacent measured facts, same census:
- `Text type="display-3"` (29px) appears at **9 sites** and every one is a **KPI numeral**, never a heading (`dashboard.tsx:571` "27.3 k", `side-gallery.tsx:35` "12k+", `theme-showcase.tsx:936,944`, `dashboard-portfolio.tsx:467,571`, `demo/Dashboard.tsx:24`, `demo/Bento.tsx:48,292`). So `--text-display-3-size` is already bound to a different, real job.
- `theme.css:707-727` ships `.astryx-text.size-2xl|3xl|4xl` but **no `.size-5xl`** — the 42px tier has no raw-size utility, i.e. it is a token with no escape hatch.

Our scale, measured adjacent ratios (`tokens.css:86-97`):

```
4xs 6 -> 3xs 7 r=1.1667 | 3xs 7 -> 2xs 8 r=1.1429 | 2xs 8 -> xs 10 r=1.2500
xs 10 -> sm 12 r=1.2000  | sm 12 -> base 14 r=1.1667
base 14 -> lg 17 r=1.2143 | lg 17 -> xl 20 r=1.1765 | xl 20 -> 2xl 24 r=1.2000
2xl 24 -> 3xl 29 r=1.2083 | 3xl 29 -> 4xl 35 r=1.2069 | 4xl 35 -> 5xl 42 r=1.2000
```

Above base the ladder is a **minor third (1.2)**, verified: every adjacent ratio from `base` up is within 2.4% of 1.2, and 14×1.2^k = 16.8, 20.16, 24.19, 29.03, 34.84, 41.8 → rounds to exactly our 17, 20, 24, 29, 35, 42. **The ladder has no gap.** heading-1 = step 3 (24), display-3 = step 4 (29), display-2 = step 5 (35), display-1 = step 6 (42). The defect is purely semantic: three consecutive steps of one honest scale are all reachable for one job.

Measured multiples of the 14px base: 24 = 1.714x, 29 = 2.071x, 35 = 2.500x, 42 = 3.000x.

**C3 — a 1.2 ratio IS capable of being a display scale, and our 42px top is already a display top.**
- Claim: the modular scale is defined as one base multiplied by one ratio; a second number off the same base is a legitimate "multi-stranded" scale, and the founders say so themselves.
  - Source: https://www.modularscale.com/ (Scott Kellum / Tim Brown, the tool's own authors)
  - Date: 2026-09-28
  - Confidence: primary
  - Quote: "One base with one ratio is a modular scale. Any more than that is a *multi-stranded* modular scale. Multi-stranded modular scales are powerful because they add more numbers to your scale, and that flexibility means it's more likely that a number from your scale will work in your project. But too many strands can dilute a scale."
- Claim: the ORIGINAL modular-scale paper, and its own counter-caveat, and — decisively — the founder's statement that the display number is chosen by eye, not derived.
  - Source: https://alistapart.com/article/more-meaningful-typography/ (Tim Brown, "More Meaningful Typography", A List Apart, 2011-05-03)
  - Date: 2026-09-28
  - Confidence: primary
  - Quote: "Double-stranded scales tend to provide more measurement options (they include more numbers, and usually fill one another's gaps)."
  - Quote: "I knew I wanted this headline to be large and have enough presence to anchor the composition, but I didn't want it to be overpowering. So I played with the text a bit, and the resulting font-size, `190px`, became my important number."
  - Quote (counter-angle — modular scale is not a law): "Modular scales are tools, they're not magic. They're not going to work for every measurement, and that's okay. Math is no substitute for an experienced designer's eye, but it can provide both hints and constraints for decision making."
  - Quote (responsive is compatible, stepping is allowed): "Designing with modular scales does not preclude grid-based and responsive design."

So the research CANNOT hand the number — it can only establish the PROCESS the kit is missing: pick ONE important number, pin it to ONE token, and forbid the neighbours. Our 24/35/42 spread is the failure of that process, not of the scale.

  - Date: 2026-09-28
  - Confidence: primary (own first-party font files)
  - Computed, not estimated — characters that fit a 640px column: JBM 42px = **25.4**, 35px = **30.5**, 29px = **36.8**, 24px = **44.4**. At 35px a JBM character is 21.00px wide; at 35px Roboto averages 17.47px.
- Claim: **the brand doctrine's stated reason is false as written.** `SKILL.md:93` says "JetBrains Mono has a small x-height; when in doubt go one role larger, never smaller." Measured x-height/upem: **JetBrains Mono 0.5500**, Inter 0.5459, Roboto 0.5283. JetBrains Mono's x-height RATIO is the **highest of the three**, not the smallest. Cap-height/upem is likewise highest: JBM 0.7300, Inter 0.7275, Roboto 0.7109.
  - Source: same measurement as above.
  - Date: 2026-09-28

- **Consequence:** a monospace face at 35px already reads as larger than a proportional reference at 35px, and wraps 1.2x sooner. A mono brand can therefore run a numerically smaller hero and still match a proportional brand's presence. This is the reason the hero does not need to be 42px.

**C5 — the hero h1 and the KPI role cannot share a value.**
- Measured: 9 sites use `Text type="display-3"` (29px) exclusively for KPI numerals; 0 use it for a heading. `SKILL.md:78` requires "Size equals importance (hero 2x, feature wide, metrics small)" and "Hierarchy is two-tier".
- So `--text-display-3-size` (29px) is **already spoken for as the KPI role**. Pinning the hero to 29px would put the hero and the KPI at the same 29px and collapse the two-tier hierarchy the doctrine exists to protect. 29px is therefore NOT available to the hero.

**C6 — there is no 2.00x step on our scale.**
- 2.00 × 14 = 28px. Our minor-third steps near it are 24px (1.714x) and 29px (2.071x). 28px is not on the scale. This is why "hero 2x" cannot be satisfied literally without a new value.

## Rule
**The `h1` role takes ONE VALUE PER SURFACE CLASS. On document and marketing surfaces, the page `h1` is `--text-display-2-size` (`--font-size-4xl`, 35px, 2.50× the 14px base). Inside a dense app panel, a routed section title stays at `--text-heading-1-size` (24px) — 35px there is a shape failure, not a style choice. Keep `--text-display-3-size` (29px) as the KPI-numeral role only, and never add a fourth display tier.**

**THE CLASSIFIER, stated explicitly because a rule that cannot state its own classifier gets applied to the next case by resemblance — and resemblance is what a size check or a name check gives you.** WCAG 2.5.8 (Level AA) is the normative precedent: *"The size of the target for pointer inputs is at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels, except when: … Inline — The target is in a sentence or its size is otherwise constrained by the line-height of non-target text."* An inline link may be 16px, fit its layout, and not be a violation, because it belongs to a different class. Ours should ship its classifier the same way.

Classify by **what the heading is the title of**, never by what it contains:

| | Document / marketing surface | Dense app panel |
|---|---|---|
| **the h1 is the title of** | the page or document itself | a routed record or section *inside* a page that also carries other controls |
| **value** | `--text-display-2-size` (35px) | `--text-heading-1-size` (24px) |
| **worked examples** | `side-gallery.tsx:80` (marketing hero), `settings.tsx:101` (page `LayoutHeader`) | `detail-page.tsx:220` (`#1001`, under a back link), `settings-sidebar.tsx` x10 (one per `activeNav` section) |

**The classifier is a property of the SITE, and this wording is load-bearing.** It must never be phrased as "a heading short enough to fit at the smaller size" — that reintroduces the exact bug this rule exists to fix, and it will read as correct. `detail-page.tsx:220` is `#1001`, five characters, 105px wide at 35px, and it is **still correct at 24px** because it sits in a `VStack gap={0}` under a back link. A string that fits is not the question. The test is whether the heading titles the page, or titles something the page contains.

**Why surface class and not role, and why this was corrected twice.** The first version of this rule said "marketing-page hero h1," but the census measured all 52 `level={1}` sites including logins, settings, tables and editors — a scope mismatch that would have been applied past its scope. The second version said "the page h1 role is bound to one value," which the `settings-sidebar` outcome falsified: 10 routed page h1s correctly stay at 24px while 29 others move to 35px. **The h1 role is overloaded — it does double duty as a page's display title and as a compact route title inside a dense app panel — and no single value serves both. That overloading is the real root cause of the 24/35/42 spread.**

**This is sourced, not invented.** GOV.UK steps the *same* heading role by surface: `heading-xl` 48px on the start page, `heading-l` 36px on ordinary pages, and 48px→32px across the tablet breakpoint for the same class. M3 splits by role *and* viewport: display "work best on large screens", headline "best-suited for short, high-emphasis text on smaller screens".

**Counter-evidence against the single-value reading, stated so it is not buried:** GOV.UK — the source leaned on hardest here — uses **three** page-level heading values chosen by page role, so a kit *could* legitimately split page-h1 into a start-page h1 and an ordinary-page h1. We do not, because **before this change** our h1 role was already bound at 24px with 38 of the 52 sites sitting there, and the defect is that the display ladder is *also* reachable for that same role. Picking one of the three existing values is the minimal fix; introducing a GOV.UK-style two-value page-h1 scheme is a **new role and a new token**, `NEEDS HUMAN APPROVAL`, and beyond this angle.

## Value to adopt
`--text-display-2-size` → `var(--font-size-4xl)` = **35px**, an existing shipping token, and the token that keeps a full 1.2 step of separation from the 29px KPI role.

**The 35px choice does NOT rest on "it is what most sites do," because it is not.** In the **pre-migration** census, 24px is the **plurality** at 38/52 = 73%, and it is the smallest of the three. 35px is the plurality only among the 14 sites that chose to type their h1 explicitly (12 of 14). The case for 35px is the surface analysis above — one value per surface class, a full scale step above the KPI role, and a value that sits between Apple's 2.00x, GOV.UK's 2.53x and M3's canonical 2.81x — not a count. **No doc sentence may say "majority" or "most templates" unqualified on this point.**

No new token, no new hex, no new font, no new weight. Requires a migration (**27 in-scope untyped h1 + 2 `display-1` h1 → `display-2` = 29 sites**), not an invention. The other 11 untyped h1 sites are dense-panel titles that stay at 24px under the surface rule.

Line-height already fits: `--text-display-2-leading: 1.2571` × 35 = 44.0px line box. No leading change needed.

## Conflicts / caveats

1. **CONFLICT with brand doctrine, surfaced not smoothed.** `SKILL.md:78` says "hero 2x". 35px is 2.50×, not 2×. The only 2.07× step is 29px, which C5 shows is occupied by the KPI role. **The human must choose:** either (a) accept 35px and treat "2x" in the doctrine as "an order of magnitude above body, not an exact multiplier" — the reading this research recommends — or (b) insist on a literal 2.00x, which is 28px, which is **NEEDS HUMAN APPROVAL: new value**, is off the minor-third scale, and forces the KPI role down to 24px where it collides with heading-1. **Recommendation: (a).** (b) is strictly worse on every measured axis.
2. **CONFLICT with the skill's own type rationale.** `SKILL.md:93`'s "JetBrains Mono has a small x-height" is measurably false (JBM x/upem 0.5500 > Roboto 0.5283). The instruction's conclusion survives on Apple's thin-weight rule instead. The reason should be corrected in the same pass or the next agent will size by a fact that points the other way. This is a doctrine-truthfulness fix, not a token change.
3. **Apple is the only 2.00x system and it is not a modular scale.** Apple's Large Title = exactly 2.00x body on both platforms, but Apple's ladder is a hand-set text-style table, not a ratio ladder. So Apple's 2.00x is evidence about the ROLE (one top tier) and about the MULTIPLE (2x), not evidence that 2.00x is derivable from a modular scale. It is not: our scale has no 2.00x step (C6).
4. **M3 is 3 tiers; we ship 3 tiers; we should ship 1.** M3 can afford 3 display tiers because each is bound to a distinct, documented job and M3 names medium the canonical one. Our `display-3` is bound to a job OUTSIDE display (KPI numerals), which leaves display-1 and display-2 both competing for the same job — the exact failure GOV.UK avoids by marking its oversize point "exceptional circumstances". Shipping 3 hero-capable tiers is the defect, not the fix.
5. **Fluid / breakpoint display type is a real, separate, UNDECIDED gap.** GOV.UK steps the SAME role by breakpoint (heading-xl = 48px large screens, 32px small screens) and Carbon ships a separate `fluid-display-01` that grows 42 → 76px. Our kit has **no breakpoint stepping on type at all** and no clamp()/fluid token. Adopting a single 35px hero is compatible with adding fluid stepping later (Brown: modular scales "do not preclude grid-based and responsive design"), but the fluid range is a NEW value range and is **NEEDS HUMAN APPROVAL: new value** — not decided here.
6. **`display-1`'s retirement has a cost.** 2 h1 sites (`documentation.tsx:238`, `form-two-column.tsx:105`) are the largest hero in the kit today. If the human wants a top-of-range option, the GOV.UK precedent is to keep it but mark it "exceptional circumstances" in the skill, not to leave it silently reachable from 2 templates. Note also `theme.css` has no `.astryx-text.size-5xl`, so 42px currently has no raw-size escape hatch — retiring it costs nothing.
7. **`display-3` at 29px as KPI is NOT part of this finding** and should not be touched by it. It is a correct, distinct, working role.

## Gaps

- **Apple's SF Pro could not be measured** — it is not in an open binary I can fetch, so I have no advance-width number for it. Apple's 2.00x Large Title/body ratio is taken from their published point table, which is authoritative for the ratio but does not let me compare SF's width to JetBrains Mono's. The width argument for our kit therefore rests on Roboto and Inter, not on SF.
- **The 34pt iOS Large Title figure is at the default "Large" Dynamic Type setting.** The page I retrieved renders the `xSmall` table explicitly at 31pt; the default Large value of 34pt is Apple's standard Dynamic Type table and I did not see it printed in the retrieved section. I am reporting the 2.00x relationship (34/17 and 26/13) as Apple's published ratio and flagging that the 34pt cell itself should be re-checked against the default-Large table before it is quoted to the human as a literal. **The macOS 26pt/13pt = exactly 2.00x pair IS printed verbatim in the table I retrieved** and does not depend on this.
- **Fluent 2 and Polaris were not reached.** I ran out of turns before fetching them; no claim in this file depends on either. If the human wants a 4th/5th system, those are the outstanding two.
- **No primary source states an absolute hero px value for a monospace brand.** Every system cited is proportional. The 35px recommendation is therefore a composition of three measured facts (one-role-per-job, one-scale-step above the KPI role, monospace advance 1.2x) and the brand doctrine's two-tier rule — it is a defensible derivation, not a quoted standard. Tim Brown is explicit that this class of number is chosen by eye. **The human should eyeball 35px on a real dark surface before ruling.**
- Whether `--text-display-1-size` should be deleted outright or retained as a documented "exceptional circumstances" point (GOV.UK precedent) is a kit-scope decision this angle does not make.

### Exception 1 — `settings-sidebar.tsx` all 10 h1 sites: OUT OF SCOPE, not heroes
Conceded to PonytailAudit2 after independently verifying their structural claims: the file has **zero `level={2}`** and 13 `level={3}` at `:369, :377, :385, :434, :463, :706, :726, :741, :786, :836, :867, :880, :893`; exactly one title renders at a time; each string is a selection echo of the nav item just clicked, not a page title.

**The size math settles it independently of the semantics:** at the measured 0.600em advance, 35px = 21.0px/char, so a ~260px panel fits **12.4 chars/line**. "Professional hosting tools" (26 chars) → 3 lines; "Languages & currency" (24) → 2; "Login & security" (20) → 2. A 2-3 line display title above a settings form is the wrong shape at any verdict — this is a shape failure, not a taste call.

**Disposition: out of scope for the 35px ruling — NOT migrated, and NOT "left behind at 24px."** The correct description is narrower than either: these 10 are out of scope **because they are not display-size heroes on a document/marketing surface**, not because they are not the page h1. They **retain `level={1}`** and **retain 24px** (`--text-heading-1-size`), which is the correct value for their surface class. The file's h2 gap is closed separately, by the 13 group headings moving `level={3}` → `level={2}` — a different lens, unrelated to this ruling. Owner: PonytailAudit2.

**Resolved, not open:** PonytailAudit2's initial h1→h2 retag proposal is **withdrawn**. Landing it alongside the 13 group headings going h3→h2 would have filled the h2 gap twice and flattened the ladder. Option (A) was taken: the 10 stay h1 (the page is routed, so exactly one renders and it matches the route name — the normal case), and the 13 group headings carry the h2. Verified independently: 10 h1, 0 h2, 13 h3, 0 h4 anywhere in the file, so after the change the file is 10 h1 + 13 h2 with nothing nested deeper.

### Exception 2 — `detail-page.tsx:220`: OUT OF SCOPE, dense-panel route title
Classified by UIAuditContentA11y reading the source; verified independently. The site is `<Heading level={1} maxLines={1}>#1001</Heading>` — the entity ID of the record being viewed, inside a `VStack gap={0}` directly beneath a `Link` with an `ArrowLeft` icon reading "All orders". That is a **routed panel title on a dense app surface**, so under the surface-class rule it stays at `--text-heading-1-size` (24px).

**This is the case that proves v3 was necessary rather than cosmetic.** A 5-character string would have *fit* at 35px (105px wide), so the size argument does not exclude it — the **surface** does. Under v2 ("the page h1 role is bound to one value") this site had no correct classification at all, because v2 had no answer for a routed panel title that legitimately keeps `level={1}`. Adding it to the exception list is what exposed the gap.

**Contrast with Exception 1, which is worth keeping distinct:** this file's heading ladder is **complete** — 1 `h1` (:220), 3 `h2` (:334, :403, :501), 3 `h3` (:574, :584, :597), with no skipped level and nothing nested deeper. `settings-sidebar.tsx` by contrast has a **gap** (10 `h1`, 0 `h2`, 13 `h3`). So the two dense-panel exceptions are *not* the same defect: one is a correctly-built ladder at the right size, the other is a mis-built ladder that happens to be at the right size. Only the second carries a ladder defect.
